About the workshop

The Family Skills Workshop teaches simple, research-based skills that help families communicate better, handle disagreements more calmly, and stay connected through stressful seasons. It is designed for parents, couples, and caregivers at any stage, including parents and their young adult children (ages 18 to 30) who are learning to relate to each other in a new way.

This is learning, not counseling. No one receives a diagnosis, and you will never be asked to share personal stories. Every activity is optional. Parents and young adults learn the same tools side by side, practice them together, and leave with a plan they can start using the same day.

The content draws on family science and peace-building coursework at BYU-Idaho, including PEACE 101 and FAML 420, along with the work of researchers such as John Gottman, Froma Walsh, and Daniel Siegel.

Who should attend: parents of children ages 5 to 18, parents of young adults, young adults ages 18 to 30, married couples, fathers and father figures, and grandparents or other caregivers.

What you will learn

  • Why conflict happensHow family systems, stress, and life changes shape the way family members react to each other.
  • How to communicate with respectReflective listening and "I" statements instead of blaming or jumping to conclusions.
  • How to solve problems as a teamRepairing after an argument instead of fighting or shutting down.
  • How to build lasting habitsA written plan of daily check-ins, weekly rituals, and repair strategies.

Not therapy

A friendly, judgment-free class. You learn and practice skills; no one is assessed or diagnosed.

Before a crisis

A proactive step for families who want to grow stronger now, much like a fitness class for relationships.

Practiced together

Unlike videos or books, you rehearse each skill with a partner or family member and get coaching on the spot.

The sessions

Each session pairs a short teaching segment with guided practice. Attend all three for the full experience, or choose the sessions that fit your family best.

Session 1

Communication Foundations: Talking and Listening with Respect

Date Saturday, October 17, 2026 Time 10:00 to 11:30 a.m. Location Madison Library, Community Room

Learn why conflicts happen between family members, especially as children grow into independent adults, and practice the core skills of calm, respectful communication.

Learning goals

  • Explain what a family system is and how one person's stress affects everyone.
  • Recognize common patterns that turn small disagreements into arguments.
  • Use "I" statements and reflective listening in a practice conversation.

You will leave with an "I" statement guide and one specific conversation to practice at home.

Agenda

  • 10 minWelcome and a short family scenario to set the stage
  • 20 minHow family conflict works, explained with everyday examples
  • 25 min"I" statement practice in pairs
  • 25 minGroup discussion and shared examples
  • 10 minClosing and take-home challenge
Session 2

Strengthening Father–Child Relationships Through Connection

Date Saturday, October 24, 2026 Time 10:00 to 11:15 a.m. Location Madison Library, Community Room

For fathers and father figures of children ages 5 to 18, and the co-parents and caregivers who support them. Learn how small, consistent moments of connection build a child's emotional security.

Learning goals

  • Identify three research-based practices that strengthen father–child connection.
  • Demonstrate reflective listening or "I feel, I need" statements with a partner.
  • Create a 7-day plan with two connection activities suited to your child's age.
  • Describe how warm, consistent father involvement supports long-term well-being.

You will leave with a summary of the three connection pillars, a listening guide, and your 7-Day Connection Plan.

Agenda

  • 8 minOpening reflection on a recent moment of connection
  • 18 minThe three pillars: presence, emotional attunement, and repair
  • 20 minReflective listening demonstration and pair practice
  • 15 minWriting your 7-day connection plan
  • 6 minClosing and personal commitment
Session 3

Strengthening Family Resilience Through Intentional Connection

Date Saturday, October 31, 2026 Time 10:00 to 11:30 a.m. Location Madison Library, Community Room

Resilience comes from consistent, intentional connection, not from the absence of problems. This session brings the series together and helps you design a plan your family can keep.

Learning goals

  • Apply daily check-ins, shared rituals, and repair conversations at home.
  • Understand how emotional regulation shapes the way families handle stress.
  • Design a personal 30-Day Family Resilience Action Plan.
  • Evaluate how family rituals affect relationships and children's well-being.

You will leave with a repair conversation guide, a family ritual worksheet, your 30-day action plan, and a resource list.

Agenda

  • 10 minWelcome, review, and an opening scenario
  • 20 minThe resilience framework with short video examples
  • 25 minPracticing repair conversations and designing a family ritual
  • 20 minWriting your 30-day action plan
  • 15 minClosing reflection and resources
